# Liquidity Risk and Expected Stock Returns

**Source:** Pástor & Stambaugh (2003) · Journal of Political Economy · DOI: 10.1086/374184

## TL;DR

Market-wide liquidity is a priced **state variable**. Stocks whose returns are more sensitive to aggregate liquidity shocks (high "liquidity beta") earn higher average returns — a spread of roughly **7.5% per year** between high- and low-sensitivity stocks over 1966–1999. This is *liquidity risk* (covariance with market liquidity), distinct from the liquidity *level* premium of Amihud-Mendelson.

## What anomaly it documents

- **Predictor:** a stock's liquidity beta — the sensitivity of its returns to innovations in aggregate (market-wide) liquidity.
- **Direction:** positive — stocks that fall most when market liquidity dries up must compensate investors with higher expected returns.
- **The measure:** Pástor & Stambaugh build a market liquidity series from temporary, order-flow-induced return reversals at the individual-stock level (larger volume-related reversals indicate lower liquidity), then aggregate across stocks and extract monthly innovations.
- OSAP predictor: **BetaLiquidityPS**.

## How to construct it

- **Step 1 — market liquidity series:** for each stock-month, estimate the return-reversal coefficient on signed volume; average across stocks to get aggregate liquidity; take innovations (the unexpected component).
- **Step 2 — liquidity betas:** regress each stock's excess returns on the liquidity-innovation series (with market/size/value/momentum controls) over a trailing window to estimate its predicted liquidity beta.
- **Universe:** US common stocks (1966–1999 in the paper).
- **Portfolio formation:** sort stocks into deciles by predicted liquidity beta.
- **Long / short:** long high-liquidity-beta, short low-liquidity-beta.
- **Weighting / rebalancing:** value-weighted, annual reformation.

- The headline liquidity-risk premium (~7.5% annually) is large and survives controls for market, size, value, and momentum.
- Liquidity risk also helps explain part of momentum — momentum winners tend to have higher liquidity-beta exposure.
- The result is **construction-sensitive**: the liquidity measure is involved and noisy, so replications vary with estimation choices (this is the same paper whose JPE indexing tripped up our automated resolver — it had to be added manually).

## Why it might work

- **Systematic liquidity risk (the thesis):** investors dislike assets that perform poorly precisely when market-wide liquidity evaporates (when cash is most valuable and selling is costly), so those assets carry a risk premium. This is a clean risk-based ICAPM-style story.
- **Flight to liquidity:** in crises, capital flees to liquid assets, so high-liquidity-beta stocks crash together — a correlated, undiversifiable risk.
- **Relation to level effects:** complements Amihud-Mendelson (1986) and Amihud (2002), which price the *level* of illiquidity; here it is the *covariance* with aggregate liquidity that is priced (formalized further by Acharya-Pedersen 2005).

## Limitations and risks

- **Estimation noise:** liquidity betas are second-order objects estimated on a constructed series — noisy and unstable, producing turnover and replication disagreement.
- **Measure dependence:** results depend on the specific reversal-based liquidity proxy; alternative liquidity measures give different betas.
- **Overlap:** entangled with size, the illiquidity-level premium, and momentum.
- **No free full text:** paywalled; see DOI (working-paper versions circulate via NBER/author pages).
